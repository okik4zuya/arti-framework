## Title Style
- **Typical word count range:** 12-20 words.
- **Structural formula:** "[Topic]: a scoping review and bibliometric analysis of [scope]" or "[Topic] in [context]: [what was mapped]".
- **Novelty in title:** Implied (the scope and method are named, no claim of "first").
- **Common punctuation patterns:** One colon at most; no question marks.

## Abstract Style
- **Total word count range:** 180-250 words.
- **Sentence count per component:** Background: 2 / Objective: 1 / Methods: 2 / Results: 3-4 / Conclusion: 2
- **Dominant tense:** Past for methods and results, present for background and conclusions.
- **Voice:** Mixed, passive for methods ("records were screened"), active for results ("the corpus grew").
- **How results are quantified:** Specific counts and shares ("112 of 148 records", "63%"), one or two key numbers, never "many" or "most" without a figure.

## Voice & Tone
- **Overall register:** Formal, plain, no rhetorical questions, no first person singular.
- **Passive vs. active voice ratio:** About 60% active in Introduction and Discussion, about 70% passive in Methods.
- **Hedging language patterns:** "suggests", "appears to", "is consistent with", "may reflect", "in this corpus", "within the limits of abstract-level evidence".
- **Claim qualification style:** Conservative. Every claim is tied to a count or a figure; claims about what the literature "lacks" are limited to "in the records screened here".

## Section-Level Patterns

**Introduction:**
- Paragraph count: 4 (context, what is known, gap, objective with the research questions).
- How gap is stated: One sentence of the form "However, no overview of X in terms of Y is available."
- How objective is introduced: Last paragraph, "This scoping review therefore maps ... (RQ1-RQ3)."

**Methods:**
- Level of procedural detail: Enough to repeat the search: database, exact query string, date, filters, screening criteria, tools by name.
- Tense and citation of instruments: Past tense; software and databases named with version or date.

**Results and Discussion (separate sections):**
- How figures are introduced: "Figure 2 shows ..." followed by one number; every figure and table is cited in the text before it appears.
- How literature comparisons are framed: Narrative citations for named findings ("Lee (2024) reported ..."), parenthetical for support.
- How contradictory results are handled: Stated in one sentence with both sources, followed by a possible reason.

**Conclusion:**
- Paragraph count: 1-2; contribution first, limitations second, one sentence on what follows.

## Vocabulary & Terminology
- Preferred terms: "records", "sources of evidence", "corpus", "included", "abstract-level", "full-text".
- Abbreviations are spelled out at first use and used at least three times afterwards, or not at all.

## In-Text Citation Habits
- Narrative vs. parenthetical: Parenthetical by default, narrative for the two or three works the paragraph is about.
- Typical references per claim: 1-3.
- Recent or seminal: Recent (last five years) first, seminal only where a definition is needed.

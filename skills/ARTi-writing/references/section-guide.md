# Section Writing Guide

Guidance for drafting each standard manuscript section. Always cross-check with the Journal Profile
for section-specific word limits and structural requirements.

---

## Title
- Concise, specific, and informative
- Reflects the key finding or contribution, not just the topic
- Avoid abbreviations unless universally recognized
- Common structures: "[Method/Material] for [Application]" or "[Finding] via [Approach]"

---

## Abstract
- Summarize: background/motivation, objective, methods, key results, conclusion/significance
- No citations, figures, or undefined abbreviations
- Written last — after the full manuscript is drafted
- Match format to journal requirement (structured with subheadings vs. unstructured paragraph)

---

## Keywords
- 4–8 terms not already in the title
- Use terms that maximize discoverability (index terms, MeSH, field-standard vocabulary)

---

## Introduction
Structure:
1. Broad context — why does this topic matter?
2. Literature review — what has been done? (funnel from general to specific)
3. Research gap — what is missing or unresolved?
4. Objective/novelty statement — what does this paper do and why is it significant?

Tips:
- Each paragraph should advance the argument toward the gap and objective
- Cite recent, high-impact literature from the target journal where possible
- End with a clear statement of the paper's aim and structure

⚠️ **High plagiarism risk section.** The Introduction draws heavily on existing literature. Every sentence must reflect your own synthesis — not a rephrasing of someone else's review article or prior paper. Write from your Scratchbook notes, not directly from the source text.

---

## Materials & Methods
- Written in past tense, passive voice (common convention)
- Sufficient detail for reproducibility
- Subsections: Materials, Synthesis/Preparation, Characterization, Analysis
- Cite instrument models, software versions, and standard protocols
- Do not include results here

⚠️ **Highest plagiarism risk section.** Researchers commonly reuse Methods text from their own prior papers (self-plagiarism) or from protocol/instrument papers. This section must be written fresh for every manuscript, even if the experimental procedure is identical to prior work. If a standard protocol is referenced, cite it — do not reproduce its text.

---

## Results
- Present findings objectively, in logical order
- Each figure/table must be introduced and described in the text
- Use past tense ("XRD patterns showed...")
- Do not interpret or discuss here (unless journal uses combined Results & Discussion)

---

## Discussion
- Interpret the results in the context of the research question
- Compare with literature: agree, disagree, or extend prior findings
- Explain unexpected results
- Discuss limitations honestly
- Connect findings back to the stated objective

---

## Results & Discussion (Combined)
Used when journal requires combined section:
- Introduce data → describe → interpret → connect to literature
- Repeat per characterization technique or experimental variable
- Maintain clear paragraph structure: one idea per paragraph

---

## Conclusion
- Summarize key findings (do not repeat abstract verbatim)
- State the significance and contribution clearly
- Suggest future work (1–2 sentences)
- No new data or citations

---

## Acknowledgements
- Funding sources (grant numbers)
- Technical support, instrument access
- Follow journal's specific format

---

## References
- Follow journal citation style exactly (numbered, APA, ACS, Vancouver, etc.)
- Use reference manager (Zotero, Mendeley, EndNote) to avoid formatting errors
- Verify all DOIs are active

⚠️ **Hallucination risk.** Never accept a reference generated by Claude without manually verifying: author names, article title, journal name, year, volume, page numbers, and DOI. AI models can produce plausible-looking but entirely fabricated citations. Every reference must be confirmed against the actual source.
